## Objective

- **Do now:** Choose one IaC path, generate templates, validate them, and attempt deployment.
- **Input:** Approved requirements, architecture and cost evidence, preliminary policy feasibility when applicable, SKU manifest, and current review findings.
- **Output:** Governance constraints, approved IaC plan and contracts, one IaC track, validation evidence, deployment summary or actual blocker, and workflow diagram.
- **Required to move on:** Governance reconciliation and approval, approved plan review, one validated IaC path, explicit scoped deployment authorization before any attempt, a recorded deployment attempt or blocker, and the workflow explanation.
- **Decisions now:** Bicep vs Terraform, module boundaries, governance handling, and what evidence to keep.
- **Next:** C4 branches from your actual deployment outcome and extends the same platform for HA/DR.

This is the most execution-sensitive step in the sequence. Keep the work moving by
choosing one path, validating often, and recording what happened.

## The Business Challenge

Nordic Fresh Foods now needs production-ready infrastructure code that can be deployed
consistently, respects governance and security controls, and stays maintainable for a
small DevOps function. A design that looks good on paper but cannot be validated or
deployed is not enough.

## Your Tasks

In the VS Code Copilot harness, use `01-Orchestrator` to delegate these bounded
owner tasks, or select the needed domain owners directly. Owners invoke the shared
independent `challenger-review-subagent` where required and the authorized parent
records the actual result. If a delegated owner cannot launch the reviewer,
Orchestrator can relay the same review as an independent sibling. `10-Challenger` is
an optional manual wrapper, not a required handoff.

1. **Discover governance (~5 min).** Have `04g-Governance` reuse current authorized preliminary evidence or discover effective subscription and inherited policy constraints in `04-governance-constraints.md` and `.json`. Complete any required independent reconciliation review and the Governance approval. Only complete discovery can establish that no constraints apply.
2. **Plan the implementation (~8 min).** Have `05-IaC Planner` create `04-implementation-plan.md` and its machine-readable contracts. The Planner invokes the independent comprehensive review; resolve blocking findings, verify the recorded review is current, and approve the plan and contracts.
3. **Generate one IaC track (~10 min).** Have `06b-Bicep CodeGen` or `06t-Terraform CodeGen` implement the approved track. Keep the plan and contracts read-only and retain `05-iac-handoff.json`. Code review is optional; deterministic validation is not.
4. **Validate and preview (~7 min).** Run the selected track's deterministic checks and Bicep what-if or Terraform plan. Review and accept the exact current preview, then obtain the matching policy precheck. Validation and preview acceptance do not authorize deployment.
5. **Authorize and deploy (~10 min).** Review the current preview, policy precheck, tenant, subscription, environment, operation, phase, budget, and matching code and parameters. If the checks permit apply, separately authorize the exact scope as a team before `07b-Bicep Deploy` or `07t-Terraform Deploy` changes Azure. Record the actual result in `06-deployment-summary.md`; if a gate blocks or deployment fails, preserve the exact blocker instead of claiming success.
6. **Explain the compressed workshop path (~5 min).** Create `03-des-deployment-workflow.md` showing bounded Orchestrator delegation and results or missing-input requests returning to it, or direct-owner entry. Include independent reviews and human stage gates, CodeGen and the IaC handoff, validation, the exact preview and policy precheck, separate human deployment authorization, actual results, and repair paths back to the owner of each blocker.

## Key Decisions

- Which IaC language gives your team the best chance of producing maintainable,
  reviewable code inside the workshop timebox?
- Which resources or concerns deserve separate modules, and which can stay simple in
  the first version?
- Which governance and security requirements must be built into the templates before
  you even try deployment?
- What evidence best proves that the templates are real, validated, and understood by
  the team?

## Deliverables

- `agent-output/freshconnect/04-governance-constraints.md` and `.json`
- Governance reconciliation evidence when required by the current constraints and workflow.
- `agent-output/freshconnect/04-implementation-plan.md` and its machine-readable `04-iac-contract.json`, `04-policy-property-map.json`, and `04-environment-manifest.json`.
- Current plan review evidence with no unresolved blocking findings.
- IaC folder at `infra/bicep/freshconnect/` or `infra/terraform/freshconnect/`.

## Success Criteria

| Focus | What good looks like | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Approved implementation strategy | The plan connects approved architecture, cost, SKU, and governance evidence to code generation | Plan contracts and recorded independent review evidence are current |
| Maintainable IaC | One selected track is structured for readability and reuse | The IaC folder follows the approved plan and includes the handoff artifact |

## Watch Out

- Governance discovery is not optional because the challenge is time-boxed.
- A missing, partial, stale, or failed Azure policy query does not prove that no constraints exist.
- `05-IaC Planner` owns plan corrections. CodeGen must not silently rewrite the approved plan.
- A missing or failed review or validation is not success, and a policy precheck `BLOCK` cannot be approved away. Generic approval, preview acceptance, and automatically answered forms do not grant human permission to apply.
- Preserve the exact preview, policy, deployment, and application-health limits in the summary.
- If an affected gate cannot pass, record the blocker and keep any C4 paper path distinct from completed work.
- Do not skip artifact names; later challenges and facilitator review depend on them.
